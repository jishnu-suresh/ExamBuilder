**Life in a Totalitarian Country**
By Leigh Dekle
2017

*This informational text is about what makes a country totalitarian, and what citizens experience living under this kind of controlling government. As you read, take notes on how people are treated in totalitarian countries.*

**Imagine…**
[1] When you get up in the morning, you get dressed quickly and leave the house before the sun rises. You go to your job, which was assigned to you by someone in charge of the area you live in. You work in a factory that makes the flag of your country. You only talk about how happy you are to be at work, and you tell the other people who work with you that you are grateful for the chance to help the country.

[diagram: photo – “North Korea - Pyongyang” by (stephan) is licensed under CC BY-SA 2.0.]

When you finally go home, you are tired, but you don’t tell anyone. You don’t want anyone to think you are complaining. At home, you watch television, and the show that night is a recording of a ceremony that took place when the Supreme Leader visited a village. Your mother gets very excited to hear him speak about how your country is the best in the world. You go to sleep after eating a simple meal provided by the government. You wake up and do it all again the next day.

**Qualities of a Totalitarian Country**
Did you like the life described in the first two paragraphs? The answer is probably a very loud “NO!” If you were a person who lived in a totalitarian country, your life would probably be a lot like what was described. A totalitarian country is a country where the government controls everything and requires all people to comply$^1$ with strict rules. The citizens are oppressed$^2$ and the rights they deserve as humans are not protected.

Totalitarian countries are usually ruled by a single very powerful leader known as a dictator. Sometimes the people in the country might call their leader “Father.” This leader gets to decide who else will help him run the government — usually he picks out people who he thinks are the most loyal.

[5] You might think everyone who lives in these countries would leave, but they do not. They do not leave because the government will punish anyone who tries to flee.$^3$ Anyone who tries to speak out against the rules created by the government is also punished — they might be sent to a prison camp and forced to work, or they might even be put to death. This creates fear amongst the people and discourages anyone from doing anything that could be seen as disrespecting government. Some people don’t even realize how bad things are in their country.

**Examples of Totalitarian Countries**
You might think that it would be impossible for countries like this to exist in the world, but there have been several in history. During the years of 1933 to 1945, Germany was ruled by Adolf Hitler and the Nazi party,$^4$ and this government had many qualities of a totalitarian government. The Nazis punished people who didn’t agree with their racist ideas, and they murdered over 10 million people who they thought were inferior.$^5$ Even though the Nazis did terrible things, many people were convinced by propaganda$^6$ to support the government.

An example of a totalitarian country that exists today is North Korea. North Korea is a small country that borders China and South Korea. All of the leaders of North Korea come from one family, and the current leader is Kim Jong-un. The government of North Korea tells everyone that they must work together so that their nation can be independent of other countries. By teaching everyone in the country to care about unity, the government has been able to convince the people not to trust the outside world. For example, children in North Korea are taught to hate the United States when they go to school.

Even though the people of North Korea might seem very united, there are some people who try to rebel against the government. These people sometimes escape the country and tell their stories, but other times they are arrested and then they “disappear.” People who escape from North Korea describe horrible things, like running out of food and not being able to speak freely.

**Vocabulary**
$^1$ **Comply** (verb) to do what you have been asked or ordered to do
$^2$ **Oppress** (verb) to keep someone in a lowly position with a lot of hardship
$^3$ **Flee** (verb) to run away from a place or dangerous situation
$^4$ **Party** (noun) in politics and government, a “party” is a group of people who support the same ideas or leaders
$^5$ **Inferior** (adjective) lower in rank, status, or quality
$^6$ **Propaganda** (noun) ideas or statements that are often false or exaggerated and that are spread to support a cause, political leader, or government

Read “Life in a Totalitarian Country” by Leigh Dekle on the previous pages. Then answer the questions below. Circle the best answer. Each question is worth 1.5 points.

1. What is the central idea of the text? [1.5] {mcq}
a) Totalitarian governments give citizens many choices about how to live.
b) Totalitarian governments control people’s lives and limit their freedoms.
c) Totalitarian governments are always supported by every citizen.
d) Totalitarian governments mainly focus on improving education.
2. According to the text, why might people be afraid to speak out against their government? [1.5] {mcq}
a) They may lose their jobs because they are not working hard enough.
b) They may be imprisoned, forced to work or even killed.
c) They may be asked to leave the country voluntarily.
d) They may have to move to a different part of the country.
3. What is the role of propaganda in a totalitarian country, according to the text? [1.5] {mcq}
a) It encourages people to question the government’s decisions.
b) It gives citizens access to different political opinions.
c) It persuades people to support the government and its ideas.
d) It allows citizens to choose their own leaders.
4. Why does the author begin by asking readers to imagine an ordinary day in a controlled country? [1.5] {mcq}
a) To help readers picture what life under totalitarian rule might feel like.
b) To explain how people can apply for a government job.
c) To show that all citizens enjoy the same daily routine.
d) To introduce the history of North Korea.
5. Which detail from the text best shows that citizens have limited freedom? [1.5] {mcq}
a) The country has a leader who visits villages.
b) People are assigned jobs and punished for trying to flee or disagree.
c) Families watch television together in the evening.
d) Children attend school and learn about other countries.
6. How does fear help a totalitarian government maintain control? [1.5] {mcq}
a) It encourages citizens to openly debate government decisions.
b) It makes citizens more likely to challenge the rules.
c) It discourages people from speaking out or resisting the government.
d) It allows citizens to decide which laws they want to follow.
7. Which feature of the totalitarian country described in the text is also present in Jonas’s Community in *The Giver*? [1.5] {mcq}
a) Citizens are encouraged to make their own important life choices.
b) Leaders and rules control important parts of people’s lives.
c) People can freely express every emotion and opinion.
d) Citizens can ignore all community rules without consequences.
8. What is one important difference between the society described in the article and Jonas’s Community? [1.5] {mcq}
a) Jonas’s Community uses rules and control to create sameness and order, while the article describes control through a powerful political leader and punishment.
b) Jonas’s Community has no rules, while the article describes a society with rules.
c) The article describes a society where everyone has freedom, while Jonas’s Community does not.
d) Jonas’s Community allows everyone to choose their job, while the article does not.
9. In both the article and *The Giver*, how can controlling information affect people? [1.5] {mcq}
a) It helps everyone understand different points of view.
b) It can prevent people from questioning what they are told and limit their understanding of the world.
c) It guarantees that citizens will always know the truth.
d) It encourages people to travel and discover other societies.
10. Which statement best compares the two texts? [1.5] {mcq}
a) Both show that a society can use control to limit individual freedom, even when that control is presented as necessary for the community.
b) Both show that citizens have complete freedom to make personal decisions.
c) Both argue that strict rules always make people happier.
d) Both describe societies where the government has very little influence over daily life.
